# P2.4, real debt minimums: the design

2026-10-04. Written before any code, per the brainstorming gate. Every figure
below was read out of `app/lib` or computed from the seed, not recalled.

## The defect

`app/lib/core/money/safe_to_spend.dart:93`:

```dart
final double debtMinimums = math.max(0, debtsIOwe * 0.08);
```

Eight percent of every peso you owe is held back from your spendable money,
every cycle, whoever you owe it to and whatever you actually have to pay. The
Safe to Spend sheet then prints that number on screen, labelled "Debt minimums
(borrowings and loans)", so the app shows its own working and the working is a
guess.

It is the second half of the finding P2.3 closed the first half of: "counts
emergency savings as spendable **and** reserves 8 percent of all debt,
including family utang with no minimum".

## It is wrong in BOTH directions, which the roadmap did not say

This is the finding that changes the design, and it came from reading the seed
rather than from the review.

The two debts in the sample ledger that you owe are BOTH
`DebtSchedule.scheduled` and both carry `installmentCurrent` and
`installmentTotal`. The app already knows what they cost every month:

| Debt | Remaining | Instalment | Left | Real monthly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home Credit (Phone) | 7,350 | 3 of 6 | 3 | 2,450 |
| BPI Personal Loan | 10,000 | 2 of 6 | 4 | 2,500 |
| | **17,350** | | | **4,950** |

Eight percent of 17,350 is **1,388**. The real figure is **4,950**.

So on the shipped demo the engine **under-reserves by 3,562** against debts
that have a genuine monthly payment. The story everybody has been telling, that
the 8 percent over-charges people for family utang, is also true, but it is the
smaller half and it is not what the sample data shows.

Both failures come from the same cause: a percentage of a BALANCE is not a
monthly payment. It is too small for a short loan and too large for a debt that
has no schedule at all.

## The shape

```
For each unsettled debt you owe:

  1. The person entered a minimum        -> use it
  2. Otherwise it is `scheduled` with    -> remaining / instalments remaining
     instalment numbers
  3. Otherwise                           -> RESERVE NOTHING
```

Rule 3 is the heart of it. Salapify never assumes a percentage, which is the
rule `health_check.dart` already states in its own words and the rule
`docs/DEFERRED.md` already recorded as waiting for you.

One new optional field, `Debt.minimumPayment`, additive on the wire and written
only when set, exactly like `Account.purpose` in P2.3. No migration, no schema
bump, nothing re-interpreted in anybody's stored file.

## What moves, measured

On the shipped demo, conservative:

| Figure | Today | After |
|---|---|---|
| Debt minimums reserved | 1,388 | 4,950 |
| Safe to spend until payday | lower by about 3,029 | |

That is the headline going DOWN, because the app was under-reserving against
two real loans. For somebody whose only debt is family utang the number goes
UP instead, because nothing is reserved against a debt with no schedule.

Both directions are the correct figure appearing, and both will surprise
somebody, so the Safe to Spend sheet has to say which rule produced the number.

## The golden lock, and how it survives

The 8 percent is the prototype's, at `src/utils/safeToSpendEngine.ts:57`, and
the vectors lock it. P2.3 already proved the way through this: the parity group
runs the fixture in the state the TypeScript can represent, and the new
behaviour gets its own generated vectors.

Here the degenerate case is a ledger where no debt has a minimum and no debt is
scheduled, which reserves nothing, and the TypeScript cannot express that
either. So the parity vectors are regenerated the same way as vector D: by
running the untouched engine with `debtsIOwe` set to the value each rule
produces, which is a legitimate input change rather than an edit to the engine.

## What does NOT change

- `debtsIOwe` itself, and every screen that shows what you owe. This changes
  what is RESERVED, never what is OWED.
- Net worth, the Debts screen, the payment register, archiving.
- The debt payoff and interest engines. Untouched.
- Instalment plans, which are already reserved in full at step 3 and are a
  separate collection from debts.

## How it is tested

1. A unit test per rule, including the one that reserves nothing.
2. A directional test: the demo's reservation moves from 1,388 to 4,950, and a
   family-utang-only ledger moves from a reservation to zero.
3. A codec round trip for the new field, absent reads as null, unknown refused.
4. A journey: add a family debt, watch the reservation NOT move, then add a
   minimum to it and watch it move by exactly that amount.
5. Break-then-prove on each.

## The one question that is yours

Everything above follows from a single rule, and it is a money-meaning rule, so
it is yours and not mine.

**When a debt has no minimum and no schedule, Salapify reserves NOTHING against
it.**

Say yes and I build the whole thing. The argument for it: an app that invents
an obligation you never agreed to is lying to you about your own money, and
"utang kay nanay" genuinely has no monthly minimum. The honest cost of saying
yes: somebody with a large flexible debt will see their Safe to Spend rise, and
they may spend money they would rather have put against that debt. The app
would be accurate and less cautious at the same time.

If you would rather it stayed cautious, the alternative is to keep a
reservation for flexible debts but make it visible and editable rather than a
hidden 8 percent. That is more work and I do not recommend it, because a number
the person did not choose is the thing we are removing.
