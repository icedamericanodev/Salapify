# P2.3, protected accounts: what was built

2026-10-04. Founder approved D1, D2 and D3 as recommended in
`docs/reviews/protected-accounts-design.md`, which this implements.

## Scope

An account can now be marked as money SET ASIDE, and money set aside stops
funding today's spending while remaining the person's in every other sense.

## The defect

Whether an account counted as spendable was decided purely by its KIND.
`liquidKinds` is `{cash, gcash, maya, bank, debit}`, and `Account.isLiquid`
was `liquidKinds.contains(kind)` and nothing else. An emergency fund kept in
GSave, Maya Savings, SeaBank or Tonik is a `gcash`, `maya` or `bank` account,
so it was counted as this fortnight's pocket money. Two of the eleven sample
accounts are literally named Savings and had exactly this defect.

## What changed

**The model.** `AccountPurpose { spendable, protected }`, and
`Account.purpose` defaulting to spendable. Two new getters: `isSpendable`
(`isLiquid && purpose != protected`) and `purposeApplies`.

**The predicate split, not a redefinition.** `isLiquid` was doing three
unrelated jobs across eleven call sites. Only the four that mean "should this
money fund today" move to `isSpendable`:

| Site | What it drives |
|---|---|
| `core/money/safe_to_spend.dart` | the headline and the surprise buffer |
| `state/financial_state.dart` (`totalLiquidCash`) | Pan's "you can reach X today" |
| `core/money/health_check.dart` | the "will I make it to payday" indicator |
| `core/money/financial_truth.dart` | the Cash Shortfall Risk alert |

That last one does not call `isLiquid` at all, it types the kinds out again,
and it drives the loudest alarm in the app. It was the single most likely
thing to be missed.

The other seven stay on `isLiquid` and therefore provably cannot change:
`log_sheet` (pay-from AND transfer-to), `payment_sheet` (x2),
`installment_sheet` (x2), `scan_receipt_sheet`, and the history filter in
`activity_screen`. A protected account stays payable from, because an
emergency spend the app refuses to record would drift the stored balance from
the real one, and because `log_sheet` builds ONE list for both the from and
the to side, so hiding protected accounts would make it impossible to move
money INTO an emergency fund.

**Two things deliberately do not move.**

1. The cash runway keeps counting protected money. It asks how long you last
   if income stops, and the emergency fund is exactly the money that answers
   that. Excluding it cuts a saver on the sample ledger from 119 days to 102.
2. Net worth, assets, liabilities and cash equivalents key off `assetKinds`,
   never `isLiquid`, so they cannot move by construction. A test now holds
   this against a later "simplification".

**The stored shape.** `purpose` added to `accountKeys`, written only when
protected (the `isSample` pattern), read with an absent-means-spendable
default. An unknown value THROWS rather than falling back, because a future
third purpose read as "spend this" would hand somebody their emergency fund
back. `setAsideReviewedAt` added as a nullable scalar beside `onboardedAt`.
NO MIGRATION and no schema bump.

**The screens.** A Spending / Set aside picker on the account sheet, hidden
for credit, loan, mortgage, investment and receivable where it would be
inert. "Set aside" on the Accounts row meta line. A snackbar on save naming
BOTH figures and the reassurance in the same breath. A one-time review card on
Accounts. Step 1 of the Safe to Spend audit now names the amount left out.
"Emergency buffer" renamed to "Surprise buffer", and step 6 follows step 1
from "liquid cash" to "spendable cash".

## What did NOT change

- **Money math.** The engine computes identically; it asks a different
  question of the account list. Every original golden vector is unchanged.
- **Stored data for anybody who does not use this.** The key is written only
  when protected, so an untouched ledger's backup is byte for byte what it
  was.
- **Net worth and the Accounts totals.** Verified by test, not by assertion.
- **Nothing is ever inferred.** Not from the name, not from the kind. Existing
  accounts stay spendable until the person says otherwise.

## The golden vectors, and why the parity claim is still true

The parity group now runs the fixture with every account forced spendable,
which is the only state `src/utils/safeToSpendEngine.ts` can represent, since
the prototype has no concept of purpose. All fourteen original figures are
unchanged and the parity claim stays honest.

Vector D is new and was GENERATED, not hand-computed: the untouched TypeScript
was run under bun twice, once with `acc_maya` removed (arithmetically what
protecting it does to the spendable subset) and once unmodified (what the
runway must still see). Each expectation names which run locks it.

| Figure | All spendable | Seed as shipped |
|---|---|---|
| Safe to spend until payday | 38,414 | 27,359 |
| Safe to spend today | 9,604 | 6,840 |
| Surprise buffer | 16,608 | 14,313 |
| Cash runway | 119 days | **119 days** |

### Break-then-prove

Two guards, each broken deliberately and watched to fail:

    copyWith with purpose dropped, the copy the ledger makes on every
    balance change:
      Expected: AccountPurpose:<AccountPurpose.protected>
        Actual: AccountPurpose:<AccountPurpose.spendable>

    the runway switched to spendable cash:
      Expected: <59>
        Actual: <5>

The first is the one that mattered most. Without `purpose` in `copyWith`,
logging one expense from a protected account would silently un-protect it,
and the person's Safe to Spend would jump back up with nothing on any screen
to explain it.

## One defect found and NOT fixed here

`financial_state.dart` sums `a.balance.pesos`, the raw balance, where
`accounts.dart:39` states in its own words "Never sum `balance` directly
across accounts". For somebody with a USD payroll account, `totalLiquidCash`
adds dollars to pesos, and that is the figure Pan reads aloud. Out of scope,
logged as its own task. `safe_to_spend.dart` has the same line but there it
is deliberate parity with a prototype that has no currency field.

## Deferred

- The history filter in `activity_screen` uses `isLiquid`, so credit-card
  transactions have no chip. Pre-existing, unrelated, untouched.
- The Accounts row shows "Maya . Maya" (kind label then institution) for
  accounts where the two match. Pre-existing cosmetic, not touched here.

## Founder decisions

All three were answered before any code was written: D1 existing accounts
stay spendable, D2 one sample account protected rather than two, D3 the audit
line plus the one-time card. No new decision is outstanding.
