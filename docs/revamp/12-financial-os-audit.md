# Salapify as a Financial Operating System: audit before building

Answer to the founder's brief, `Salapify_v3_Financial_Operating_System_Brief.md`,
section 44, which asks for an audit and a staged plan BEFORE implementation and
says in as many words: do not make large architectural changes yet.

This document is that audit. Nothing in it has been built.

Three independent expert reviews ran against the live code first (a feature
strategist, a competitor benchmark and a financial coach). Where they contradict
the brief, or me, that is said plainly rather than smoothed over.

---

## 0. The headline, before the detail

**The brief says "we are building Salapify v3 from scratch". That premise is out
of date, and acting on it would destroy the most valuable asset in the
repository.**

`app/lib/core/money` already holds **67 pure money engines**, byte identical to
the shipped app's and verified against **34 golden fixtures** generated by
executing the original React Native code. That is the "central financial engine"
of section 5, and it is not a plan, it is done and locked.

**57 of those 67 engines are wired to nothing.**

That single number reframes the entire brief. The gap between Salapify today and
the Financial Operating System described is much smaller than the document
assumes, and it is almost entirely **presentation and wiring**, not financial
architecture.

| | |
|---|---|
| Money engines ported, tested, golden locked | 67 |
| Engines no screen consults | **57** |
| Screens shipped | 5 (Home, Ledger, Plan, Accounts, Account detail) plus the Log sheet |
| Tests passing | 430 |
| Transaction editing, account creation, backup UI | **none of them exist** |

So: P0 in the brief's roadmap is roughly 80 percent complete in the ENGINE and
roughly 30 percent complete in the APP.

## 1. Repository audit

### What exists and is solid

**The money layer.** 67 files, pure Dart, no Flutter, no network, no state. Each
is a function from stored JSON plus a date to a result. `check-engine-identical.sh`
refuses a merge if any of them drifts by a byte from the shipped app's version.
This is a genuinely strong foundation and it already satisfies the brief's
section 5 requirement that calculations be deterministic and testable, and its
section 28 requirement that no LLM performs financial arithmetic.

**The storage layer.** One encrypted local store, one choke point
(`sanitizeData`) applying forward-only migrations at schema v12, refusing a blob
from a newer schema loudly rather than silently dropping fields. Backup import
of a real v12 file round trips. This satisfies section 33's offline-first
requirement today.

**The design system.** Sixteen components, two skins, a contrast test across all
palettes, a layout-metric readability sweep, and a render harness that shoots
every screen at both brightnesses. This is ahead of where most apps are at this
stage.

**The parser.** `fastlog.dart` turns a typed Taglish line into a categorised
entry on device, with no network. The competitor review could not find this
combination shipping anywhere else: everything comparable is a cloud LLM.

### What does not exist and should alarm us

These are not roadmap items. They are below the floor, and two expert reviews
independently called them pre-launch blockers:

1. **You cannot edit or delete a transaction.** No row tap, no swipe, no search,
   no filter. `updateTransaction` exists in the engine and no screen calls it.
   This is worse for Salapify than for a competitor precisely because the parser
   guesses: fast entry with a wrong guess and no repair path accumulates silent
   garbage, and the only current remedy is restoring a backup, for which there is
   also no screen.
2. **You cannot add an account.** The Accounts empty state says "add where your
   money actually sits" and nothing in the app does it. Accounts can only arrive
   through a restored backup or the debug sample data action.
3. **There is no backup or export UI**, in an app whose entire promise is that
   data never leaves the device. That is one dropped phone from total loss, and
   reviewers will say exactly that.
4. **Three of the four Home quick actions do nothing.** They are correctly
   marked disabled to screen readers, which is the right handling of a wrong
   situation.

### The stored domain model, as it actually is

    accounts, assets, debts, payables, receivables, transactions, categories,
    recurring, goals, payments, people, notes, treats, wins, quickAdds,
    notifications, settings

Against the brief's section 31 list, what is **missing as a first class entity**:

| Brief entity | Status |
|---|---|
| Insurance, Benefit (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, HMO) | absent entirely |
| Investment | folded into `assets`, no cost basis, no allocation |
| Installment | folded into `debts`, no per-installment schedule |
| SinkingFund | absent |
| Budget | not an entity: it is `settings.monthlyLimit` plus `category.monthlyCap` |
| Scenario, Forecast, FinancialState, Insight, Risk, Recommendation | absent, and see section 3 for whether they should be entities at all |
| Document | absent |

## 3. Architecture assessment

### 3.1 The brief's section 5 warning is already a live defect

The brief warns against:

    Dashboard = calculation A
    Reports   = calculation B

That has already happened, in code written this week, and the render harness
photographed it without anyone noticing:

- **Home** says "₱1,566.63 a day until payday" — available divided by days to
  the next **payday**.
- **Plan** says "₱697.73 a day for the 20 days left this month" — remaining
  divided by days to **month end**.

Two per-day spending figures, on two screens, over two different periods, from
two different denominators. They will essentially never agree, and no test
catches it because each is individually correct. This is the single strongest
argument in the brief, and it is validated by our own screenshots.

**It is also a real product error, not just an architectural one.** A Filipino
semimonthly earner does not run out of money in month four. They run out on the
27th. The cycle that matters is the half, not the calendar month.

### 3.2 There is no financial state object, and that is the real gap

Every screen today reaches into `core/money` and calls two or three engines
directly, then arranges the results. There is no single place that says "this is
the user's financial state right now". The brief is right that this is the
architectural difference between a tracker and a system, and 3.1 is what its
absence costs.

**But the fix is smaller than the brief implies.** A `FinancialState` facade
composed FROM the existing locked engines is a few hundred lines. It does not
require rewriting the engines, changing storage, or introducing a use-case
layer. See section 4.

### 3.3 Screen-driven data is NOT a problem here

The brief's section 31 warns against `DashboardTable`, `BudgetScreenTable`. That
mistake was never made: storage is one ledger blob of financial entities, and
screens derive from it. The domain boundary is already right. The gaps are
missing ENTITIES (insurance, benefits, installments), not a wrong shape.

### 3.4 Country coupling is partially right and needs a decision

`phtax.dart`, `phcalendar.dart`, `taxdeadlines.dart`, `thirteenth.dart` are
already separate files, which is most of the way to the brief's section 11 rules
layer. What is not true yet: `commitments.dart` and others call PH holiday logic
directly rather than through a country interface, the default categories are PH
flavoured, and the currency default is PHP.

**Honest recommendation: do not build the multi-country layer yet.** It is real
architectural work whose only current beneficiary is a hypothetical second
market. Keep PH logic in named files, keep calling it directly, and revisit when
a second country is an actual plan rather than an aspiration. The brief's own
section 37 ranks technical feasibility and long-term strategic value; a rules
layer scores well on the second and poorly on everything else today.

### 3.5 Pan and AI is the one genuine architectural fork, and it is founder-gated

Sections 27 to 29 describe Pan with access to the full financial state,
explaining and contextualising in natural language, with AI used for explanation
and conversation while deterministic logic does the arithmetic. That split is
exactly right and I would not change it.

**But an LLM requires a network call, and that collides with three things this
project has committed to:** offline-first (section 33 of the brief itself),
"nothing leaves your phone" as the trust proposition the competitor review
identified as our strongest install argument in a market with a live memory of
lending-app scandals, and CLAUDE.md STOP condition 3, which makes external
transmission of personal financial data a founder decision before the work, not
at the merge.

There are three honest options and they are materially different products:

1. **No network Pan.** Template based explanation over the deterministic engine.
   Keeps the privacy claim absolute. Reads as a good app, never as magic.
2. **Opt-in cloud Pan.** Off by default, explicit consent, a visible indicator
   when data leaves, and a documented minimal payload. Keeps the claim honest
   with an asterisk.
3. **On-device small model.** Technically possible, large binary, weak results
   for this job today. Not recommended.

This needs a founder decision before any Pan work. It is not mine to assume.

### 3.6 Financial health score: the brief is right to be suspicious of it

Section 10.10 asks for a transparent score and warns against a meaningless
gamified number. I would go further: **a single 0 to 100 number is the most
requested and least useful thing on the list.** It compresses seven incomparable
things into one figure that cannot drive an action, and the brief's own section
40 test asks whether a feature produces an actionable outcome.

Recommendation: build the **seven component readings** (cash flow, emergency
fund, debt, spending control, savings, investments, protection) as sentences
with numbers, and the "biggest issue plus recommended action" line. Skip the
composite score, or derive it last, once the components have been used for a
while and we know what a 78 would mean.

## 4. Proposed architecture

Minimal change, maximum compliance with the brief's section 5.

    PRESENTATION  (features/, unchanged shape)
            │  reads exactly one thing
            ▼
    FinancialState        <-- NEW, a facade, a few hundred lines
            │  composes, never computes
            ▼
    core/money  (67 engines, GOLDEN LOCKED, untouched)
            │
            ▼
    core/data   (one ledger, sanitizeData, encrypted, untouched)

**`FinancialState` is a composition layer, not a calculation layer.** It calls
the locked engines and exposes one coherent answer per question. The rule that
makes it work: **a screen may not call an engine directly.** Then 3.1 becomes
impossible by construction rather than by vigilance, because "days left in the
cycle" is defined once.

What it exposes, all from engines that already exist:

    state.cycle          the payday cycle, its start, end, days left
    state.liquid         spendable cash, with savings excluded by PURPOSE
    state.commitments    bills, minimums and dues inside the cycle
    state.safeToSpend    the hero figure and its pace, over the CYCLE
    state.netWorth       assets, liabilities, and the parts
    state.debt           both directions, with aging
    state.budget         limit and per category, paced over the CYCLE
    state.forecast       the day by day running balance and the cliff date
    state.nextAction     the single ranked do-next item

The last two are `cashflow_calendar` and `coach`, already built and unused.

**Deliberately NOT adopted from the brief:** a use-case layer, a repository
interface per entity, and Scenario / Forecast / Risk / Recommendation as stored
ENTITIES. Forecasts and risks are derived, not stored; persisting them creates a
cache to invalidate and a second source of truth, which is the exact problem
section 5 exists to prevent. Scenarios are the one arguable case, and only if
users want to save and compare them.

## 5. Domain model recommendations

Add, in this order, and only when the feature that needs it is being built:

1. **`purpose` on an account** (spendable or protected), independent of `kind`.
   This is a correctness fix, not a feature: `liquidKinds` currently keys off
   `kind`, and a huge share of this audience keeps savings in GSave inside
   GCash, or Maya Savings, SeaBank, Tonik. Added once as an e-wallet, an
   emergency fund is silently counted as today's pocket money. That is the exact
   failure the savings exclusion exists to prevent, failing silently.
2. **`dueDate` on a receivable**, because a lend with a date is a loan and a lend
   without one is a gift nobody admitted to.
3. **Installment schedule** on a debt, for section 10.7.
4. **Insurance and Benefit entities**, for section 12 and the protection reading.
5. **Investment** with cost basis, only if allocation visibility (section 23) is
   actually built.

Every one of these is a stored data change and therefore founder-gated under
CLAUDE.md STOP condition 2, individually, before the work.

## 6. Roadmap, restated honestly

The brief's P0 to P4 is, as written, multiple years. Here is the same ambition
sequenced against what exists.

### P0. Make it trustworthy and complete. NOT already done.

The brief assumes P0 is the foundation to build. In the engine it largely is.
In the app it is not, and nothing above it can stand up without this.

- Transaction detail, edit and delete. Below the floor today.
- Add and edit an account, with the `purpose` flag.
- Backup and restore UI.
- `FinancialState` facade, and the cycle contradiction fixed through it.
- Upcoming, Debt and Goals screens (already roadmap steps 6 to 8).
- Onboarding that captures rent, utilities, remittance and subscriptions, which
  is what makes safe to spend honest rather than flattering.

### P1. Wire what is already built.

Cheap, because the arithmetic is done and locked.

- Cash cliff: the date your cash runs out, from `cashflow_calendar`.
- Can I afford this: from `afford` and `bnpl`.
- Do next: from `coach`.
- Net worth history: from `net_worth_history`.
- Spending breakdown with a conclusion sentence: from `spending_breakdown`.

### P2. The differentiator, properly shaped.

- Reconcile, as corrected in D20: reason codes, a checked-as-of date, drift over
  time, cash first, and the word "reconcile" never in the UI.
- Set aside on payday, at the moment income is parsed.
- Utang aging with an opt-in, user-sent follow up.

### P3. Philippine depth.

13th month allocator (November), MP2 planner, family support, remittance mode,
holiday cash flow pressure. `thirteenth` and `phcalendar` already exist.

### P4. Intelligence.

Behavioural layer, scenarios, life events, proactive insights, Pan. **Gated on
the section 3.5 decision.**

## 7. Recommended first milestone

**"One truth, and a ledger you can trust."**

1. `FinancialState` facade, with the rule that no screen calls an engine directly.
2. Fix the Home and Plan cycle contradiction through it: pace everything to the
   PAYDAY CYCLE, keep `monthlyLimit` as the stored setting because people think
   in monthly rent and salary, and derive the half-cycle view above the locked
   `budgetSummary` without touching it.
3. Transaction detail, edit and delete.
4. Add and edit an account, with `purpose`.
5. Backup and restore UI.

**Why this first.** It is the brief's own section 5 made real, it removes a live
money contradiction, and it closes the three gaps that are currently below the
floor. Every later pillar reads from this facade, so building it now is what
stops P1 to P4 from each inventing their own truth. And Reconcile specifically
cannot ship before item 3, because it writes adjustment rows into a ledger
nobody can open.

## 8. Risks and trade-offs

**The ambition can quietly break the app's best quality.** Salapify's real
advantage today is precision: a hero that refuses to claim a payday nobody set,
a credit card counted at its minimum and never its balance, a receivable that
only counts when real money left the pocket. Every engine in the brief's P4
(risk, behaviour, scenarios) produces claims that are much harder to be that
precise about. **A wrong insight costs more trust than a missing one earns.**

**Scale versus a working app.** The brief describes a system. The app currently
cannot edit a transaction. If P1 intelligence is built before P0 completeness, we
get an app with a forecast engine and no way to fix a typo.

**The composite health score is the most likely thing to be built and regretted.**
See 3.6.

**Pan is a product fork, not a feature.** See 3.5. Answering it late means
building the intelligence layer twice.

**"Filipino-first" is no longer a moat.** P1SO (₱99 once), BudgetPH, BunnyWise
and IponBuddy already ship semimonthly cutoffs, daily allowance and two-way
utang. Own them; do not build the pitch on them. What IS rare: the offline
Taglish parser, the engine's refusal to state what it does not know, and the
reconcile SHAPE.

**And there is a real competitive threat to the capture story.** Pikash already
auto-captures GCash and Maya push notifications on device, offline and free.
Someone who has used it will time our three second typed log against their zero
second log. We need a deliberate answer, and "we are offline first" is not one,
because so are they.

1. **Approve or change the first milestone** in section 7.
2. **Decide Pan** per section 3.5: no network, opt-in cloud, or defer.
3. **Confirm we are NOT rebuilding from scratch**, and that the 67 golden locked
